How to Hard Refresh
Your Browser Cache

Force your browser to reload the latest version of a page by bypassing the local cache — step-by-step for every major browser.

What is a hard refresh?

A normal refresh reloads the page but may serve cached assets (CSS, JS, images) from your local disk. A hard refresh instructs the browser to ignore the cache entirely and download every resource fresh from the server — useful when a site has been updated but still appears to show an old version.

Step-by-step instructions

Google Chrome

Windows · macOS · Linux
Keyboard shortcut
Ctrl+Shift+R
or
Ctrl+F5
  1. 1
    Open the page you want to refresh in Chrome.
  2. 2
    Hold Ctrl + Shift and press R, or simply press Ctrl + F5.
  3. 3
    Chrome will reload the page, bypassing all cached resources.
💡 You can also open DevTools (F12), then right-click the reload button and choose "Empty Cache and Hard Reload" for a thorough clear.

⌘ Cmd+Shift+R
  1. 1

  3. 3
    Chrome will reload the page, bypassing all cached resources.
💡 Open DevTools (⌘ + Option + I), then right-click the reload button and choose "Empty Cache and Hard Reload" for a full cache wipe.

Apple Safari

macOS · iOS / iPadOS
Keyboard shortcut
⌘ Cmd+Option+R
  1. 1
    Open the page in Safari.
  2. 2
    Press ⌘ Cmd + Option + R to perform a hard reload.
  3. 3
    Alternatively, hold Shift and click the Reload button in the address bar.
💡 To fully empty the cache, go to Develop → Empty Caches (enable the Develop menu in Safari → Settings → Advanced → Show Develop menu).
  1. 1
    Open the Settings app on your iPhone or iPad.
  2. 2
    Scroll down and tap Safari.
  3. 3
    Tap Clear History and Website Data, then confirm.
  4. 4
    Return to Safari and reload the page.
💡 iOS Safari does not support a single-page hard refresh shortcut — clearing all website data is the recommended approach.
